Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article, Review

METHODS This systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials followed PRISMA guidelines and was prospectively registered. We searched MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, CENTRAL, and ClinicalTrials.gov from inception to August 2025. Eligible trials compared any GLP-1 RAs with placebo, standard care or active comparator for patients with any clinical indication. Pairs of reviewers conducted study selection, data extraction, and risk of bias assessments independently. We performed random-effects meta-analyses and rated certainty of evidence using GRADE.

Résultats disponibles Résumé PubMed (texte source)

Valeurs chiffrées surlignées telles qu'elles figurent dans le résumé. Elles n'ont pas été vérifiées dans le texte intégral.

RESULTS A total of 142 trials enrolling 172,050 participants met eligibility criteria. Moderate to high certainty evidence suggests GLP-1 RAs probably increase the risk of diabetic retinopathy (odds ratio (OR) 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.15 [1.03-1.28]), vitreous hemorrhage (OR 95% CI 1.64 [1.08-2.49]), need for retinal procedures (OR 95% CI 1.37 [1.07-1.75]), total retinal vascular disorders (OR 95% CI 1.54 [1.12-2.12]), including retinal vascular occlusion (2.33 [1.22-4.45]). High certainty evidence also showed GLP-1 RAs reduce glaucoma risk (0.58 [0.34-0.99]). Moderate certainty evidence suggests GLP-1 RAs likely have little to no effect on the risk of cataract (0.95 [0.85 - 1.06]) and retinal detachment (1.40 [0.87 - 2.27]). We are very uncertain about the effects of GLP-1 RAs on age-related macular degeneration (0.91 [0.15-5.45]) and ischemic optic neuropathy (2.19 [0.49-9.85]). Results were consistent in sensitivity analyses restricted to placebo- or standard-care-controlled trials, and follow-up duration did not modify the effects for any outcome.

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

CONCLUSION Moderate- to high-certainty evidence suggests that GLP-1 RAs likely result in an increase in the risk of selected retinal events, but reduce the risk of glaucoma. Given the low absolute incidence of these events, clinicians can counsel patients that the ocular risks of GLP-1 RAs are small and likely outweighed by their cardiometabolic benefits, while advising early monitoring for retinal events in those with diabetes or pre-existing retinopathy.
