Question étudiée Résumé PubMed (texte source)

INTRODUCTION Genetic testing benefits individuals and families with inherited eye diseases by facilitating accurate diagnosis, informing prognosis, enabling assessment for at-risk family members, and supporting targeted therapies. This study aimed to characterize the spectrum of inherited eye disorders and evaluate the diagnostic yield of genetic testing at a single tertiary eye care centre over a one-year period.

RESULTS Of 1325 patients evaluated, 862 (65%) underwent genetic testing. Inherited retinal disorders were the most common (n = 440, 51%), followed by cornea and anterior segment disorders (n = 107, 12.4%). Other conditions included hereditary optic nerve disorders (n = 75, 8.7%), glaucoma (n = 70, 8.1%), lenticular disorders (n = 54, 6.3%), phacomatoses (n = 25, 2.9%), ocular tumours (n = 20, 2.3%), strabismus and albinism (n = 19 and 18; ~2% each), MAC spectrum disorders (n = 10, 1.4%), and systemic syndromes (n = 22, 2.5%). Diagnostic yield was achieved in 605 (70%), while 257 (30%) remained inconclusive. Of the 463 untested families, the motivations were unascertained reasons (n = 156, 33%), financial constraints (n = 138, 30%), intent to test later (n = 90, 19%), and belief that no treatment was available (n = 79, 17%).

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

DISCUSSION Inherited retinal and anterior segment disorders constitute the most frequent genetic eye diseases. Genetic testing has enabled genotype-phenotype correlation in over two-thirds of cases. Financial constraints and limited availability of disease-modifying treatments remain key barriers for uptake of genetic testing.
