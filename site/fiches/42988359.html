PURPOSE To evaluate the incidence and baseline predictors of intraocular pressure (IOP)-lowering treatment following detection of referable glaucoma by teleretinal screening.

Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article

DESIGN Retrospective cohort study.
PARTICIPANTS Participants were derived from a safety-net teleretinal diabetic retinopathy screening program (2013-2024) and included individuals who screened positive for referable glaucoma (cup-to-disc ratio [CDR] ≥0.6 or CDR asymmetry ≥0.2) and completed in-office diagnostic evaluation.
METHODS Cumulative incidence functions were estimated for the initiation of IOP-lowering treatment, with loss to follow-up handled as a competing event. Fine-Gray models were used to identify baseline screening predictors of treatment and surgery, and to perform cross-validated risk stratification. Glaucoma diagnosis was approximated using diagnostic codes and chart review.
MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES The primary outcome was initiation of IOP-lowering treatment (medication, laser, or surgery). The secondary outcome was glaucoma surgery.

Résultats disponibles Résumé PubMed (texte source)

RESULTS 2,367 participants were included. The cumulative incidence of treatment was 19.6% (95% CI: 18.0-21.2) at Year 1 and 39.1% (36.7-41.6) at Year 5. Early treatment occurred primarily in glaucoma cases, whereas treatment accumulated longitudinally in glaucoma suspects, reaching 29.3% (25.8-32.7) by Year 5. Surgery was less common (2.2% [1.3-3.1] at Year 5). Risk stratification with baseline screening data produced meaningful separation at Year 5 (treatment: 51.2% [46.9-55.5] vs. 26.7% [22.8-30.7]; surgery: 3.8% [2.1-5.5] vs. 0.7% [0.1-1.3]). Older age (sub-distribution hazard ratio [SHR] 1.03 [1.02-1.04] per year, p<0.001), Black race (SHR 1.50 [1.21-1.87], p<0.001), and personal history of glaucoma (SHR 1.90 [1.34-2.70], p<0.001) were associated with treatment; Asian race was protective (SHR 0.71 [0.52-0.97], p=0.03). Older age (SHR 1.06 [1.03-1.09], p<0.001), worse visual acuity (SHR 5.11 [2.15-12.12] per logMAR unit, p<0.001), and screening at a hospital-based site (SHR 2.46 [1.35-4.49], p=0.003) were associated with surgery.

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

CONCLUSION Among safety-net diabetic patients who screened positive for referable glaucoma and were untreated at screening, nearly 40% initiated IOP-lowering treatment within 5 years, while few received surgery. Baseline screening characteristics enabled risk stratification of treatment and surgery. These findings address an evidence gap about longitudinal consequences of screening and suggest that its impact extends beyond detection of prevalent glaucoma to include identification of high-risk glaucoma suspects who warrant surveillance.
