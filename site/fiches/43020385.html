Question étudiée Résumé PubMed (texte source)

PURPOSE Open-angle glaucoma (OAG) is the leading cause of irreversible blindness and is highly heritable. Despite effective treatments, approximately 50% of cases remain undiagnosed. We aimed to develop and validate a clinically applicable, genetics-based risk prediction tool for OAG across diverse ancestries.

Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article

DESIGN Genetic association study and risk prediction analysis.
PARTICIPANTS More than 6 million individuals contributing genome-wide association study data from multiple ancestry groups.
METHODS We constructed a comprehensive polygenic risk score (PRS) for OAG using a multitrait modeling framework that integrated glaucoma-related traits, including intraocular pressure, optic disc parameters, and ocular hypertension. PRS performance was evaluated for OAG risk across major ancestry groups and assessed for associations with age at diagnosis, treatment escalation, surgical outcomes, and familial risk.
MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES Odds ratios (ORs) for OAG by PRS percentile, age at disease onset, absolute risk estimates, treatment escalation, glaucoma surgery, and OAG risk in first-degree relatives.

Résultats disponibles Résumé PubMed (texte source)

Valeurs chiffrées surlignées telles qu'elles figurent dans le résumé. Elles n'ont pas été vérifiées dans le texte intégral.

RESULTS In Australian individuals of European ancestry, those in the top 10% of the PRS distribution had a 10-fold increased risk of OAG compared with the remainder (OR 10.04, 95% CI 7.43-13.59), substantially outperforming our previously published PRS (OR 4.2). Similar performance was observed in U.S. European ancestry data (OR 9.09, 95% CI 5.09-16.26). In UK Biobank Europeans the OR for the top 10% versus the remainder was 4.91 (95% CI 4.25-5.69). Although performance was attenuated in other ancestry groups (OR top 10% vs. remainder: 4.01 in South Asian, 3.39 in East Asian, and ranging from 2.26 to 3.81 in African ancestry populations depending on cohort), high PRS percentiles corresponded to elevated absolute risk across all groups. On average, individuals in the highest-risk decile developed glaucoma approximately 25 years earlier than those in the lowest decile and had ∼100-fold (OR 96.83, 95% CI 52.61-178.21) higher risk. The PRS also predicted treatment escalation in early disease, prevalent and incident glaucoma surgery, and OAG risk among first-degree relatives.

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

CONCLUSIONS This enhanced PRS, available for clinical use through an accredited genotyping workflow, enables effective risk stratification for OAG across diverse ancestries, facilitating earlier identification and treatment of high-risk individuals while supporting resource rationalisation for those at low risk. Performance is strongest in European ancestry populations, and further work is needed to improve equity of performance across ancestries.
