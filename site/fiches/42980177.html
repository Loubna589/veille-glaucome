RESULTS GATT decreased IOP significantly at all postoperative follow-ups in both groups (P<0.001) with no intergroup differences (P > 0.05). GATT also reduced the requirement of anti-glaucoma agents in both groups. The PCG group required fewer medications than the JOAG group except for the 24-month time point (P<0.05). PCG had higher cumulative partial and complete success probabilities than JOAG eyes (P=0.011 and 0.002, respectively). Prior glaucoma surgery predicted surgical failure. No vision-threatening events were recorded with transient hyphema and IOP spike being the most common complications. IOP spike in one eye had predictive value for the fellow eye in PCG, but not in JOAG.

CONCLUSIONS GATT is effective and safe in our PCG and JOAG cohorts, with superior outcomes in younger, surgery-naïve eyes. In PCG, an IOP spike in the first-operated eye predicts that in the fellow eye.
