Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article

METHODS From the initial cohort of 143 eyes (86 participants), one eye per participant was included to avoid inter-eye correlation bias, comprising 58 glaucomatous eyes and 28 control eyes. All participants underwent monocular testing with a 30 Hz flickering dot stimulus mapped onto the Humphrey Visual Field 24-2 grid. Sectoral detection scores were compared with Humphrey Visual Field indices and retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thickness. Group differences were analyzed with Mann-Whitney U tests, correlations with Spearman's rho, and diagnostic accuracy with receiver operating characteristic analysis. Test-retest reliability of the mean sector score was assessed in a subset of 26 eyes, including 18 glaucoma patients and 8 control subjects, using a two-way random-effects model for absolute agreement.

RESULTS Perceptive Scotoma Finder detection proportions were markedly higher in glaucoma than in controls across all Garway-Heath sectors (e.g., IN: 85.7% vs 1.9%; all p < 0.001). Detection scores showed correlations with mean deviation (ρ = 0.29 to 0.50) and pattern standard deviation (ρ = 0.36 to 0.69; all p < 0.001), and with RNFL thickness in all sectors except the temporal sector (p = 0.252). Sensitivity ranged from 0.68 to 0.91 and specificity from 0.83 to 0.90 across sectors. Test-retest reliability, based on 26 eyes, was 0.76 (95% confidence interval 0.72-0.89) by ICC, and Bland-Altman analysis showed a mean bias of 0.00 with 95% limits of agreement from -0.24 to 0.24. Overall discriminative performance produced an area under the curve of 0.79 (95% Confidence interval 0.76-0.83).

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

CONCLUSIONS The Perceptive Scotoma Finder demonstrated reproducible, sector-specific detection of established glaucomatous visual field defects and showed moderate agreement with conventional visual field and RNFL measures. These findings support its feasibility and potential as a rapid, low-cost adjunctive tool for glaucoma assessment; however, further evaluation in early glaucoma and screening populations is required.
