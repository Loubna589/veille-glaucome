PURPOSE To compare visual outcomes and postoperative complication rates associated with different intraocular lens (IOL) implantation strategies following posterior capsule rupture (PCR) with vitreous loss during cataract surgery.

Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article

METHODS This retrospective cohort study included eyes that underwent cataract surgery complicated by PCR and anterior vitrectomy between 2012 and 2021 at a tertiary referral center. Eyes were grouped according to IOL implantation strategy: three-piece sulcus IOL (BAL-65), anterior chamber IOL (AC-IOL), or iris-fixated IOL (ARTISAN). Primary outcome was change in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) from preoperative baseline to 6 months postoperatively, after exclusion of eyes with preoperative visual-limiting pathology. Secondary outcomes included rates of postoperative complications, specifically glaucoma, corneal endothelial insufficiency, cystoid macular edema, retinal detachment, and complications leading to poor visual outcome.

RESULTS A total of 249 eyes were included (102 AC-IOL, 54 ARTISAN, 93 BAL-65). After exclusion of eyes with preoperative visual-limiting conditions, three-piece sulcus IOL implantation was associated with significantly greater visual improvement compared with both AC-IOL and Iris-fixated IOL implantation (mean ΔBCVA 1.06 vs. 0.47 and 0.54 LogMAR, respectively; p < 0.01). No significant difference in visual improvement was observed between AC-IOL and Iris-fixated IOL groups. Postoperative complications occurred more frequently in the AC-IOL group, particularly secondary glaucoma (6.9% vs. 1.9% and 1.0% in Iris-fixated IOL and sulcus IOL groups, respectively). Complications leading to poor visual outcome were significantly more common in AC-IOL compared with sulcus IOL (22.7% vs. 9.8%, p = 0.021).

CONCLUSIONS In eyes with PCR and vitreous loss, three-piece sulcus IOL implantation was associated with greater visual improvement and fewer vision-threatening complications than AC-IOL implantation, supporting sulcus placement when adequate capsular support is present.
