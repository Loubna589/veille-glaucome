Question étudiée Résumé PubMed (texte source)

INTRODUCTION Bio-reinforced cyclodialysis uses a biocompatible acellular scleral allograft spacer to maintain a surgically created cleft and enhance uveoscleral outflow. The titratable effect of one vs two spacers on intraocular pressure (IOP) lowering has not previously been evaluated.

Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article

METHODS Analysis of 260 eyes (187 patients) in the prospective, multicenter CREST study (NCT05506423) undergoing combined phacoemulsification and bio-reinforced cyclodialysis with one spacer (N = 146) or two (N = 114). The primary endpoint was IOP change from baseline to month 12. Analyses comprised unadjusted comparison, baseline-adjusted regression, 1:1 propensity score matching, doubly robust overlap weighting, and models accounting for enrolling site and fellow eyes. IOP after a secondary IOP-lowering procedure was censored. IOP reported as mean (95% CI) and medications as mean (± SD).

Résultats disponibles Résumé PubMed (texte source)

Valeurs chiffrées surlignées telles qu'elles figurent dans le résumé. Elles n'ont pas été vérifiées dans le texte intégral.

RESULTS At baseline, IOP was 17.8 (4.2) vs 18.5 (5.7) mmHg and medications 1.3 (1.1) vs 1.5 (1.4), with one vs two spacers, respectively. Enrolling site was most imbalanced: one center contributed 38.6% of two-spacer eyes but 7.5% of one-spacer eyes. The unadjusted difference in IOP reduction was 2.5 mmHg (1.2-3.8; p < 0.001) and persisted after propensity score matching (2.4 mmHg; 0.9-3.8; p = 0.001) and overlap weighting (2.0 mmHg; 1.2-2.8; p < 0.001). After accounting for site, it attenuated to 0.9 mmHg (- 0.1 to 1.8; p = 0.080), and to 0.4 mmHg (- 0.6 to 1.5; p = 0.424) using both strategies. Medication reduction (- 0.7 vs - 0.6; p = 0.643), freedom from secondary incisional surgery (98.6% vs 99.1%), and site-adjusted adverse event rates did not differ.

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

CONCLUSION Two-spacer treatment was associated with numerically greater 12-month IOP reduction in unadjusted and patient-level adjusted analyses, but this association was substantially attenuated after accounting for enrolling site. These short-term real-world data are compatible with, but do not establish, an independent incremental effect of a second spacer. Further study is warranted.
