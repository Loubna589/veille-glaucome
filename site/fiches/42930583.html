Résumé complet (non structuré)

Background/Objectives: The upfront costs of angle-based minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) maybe offset by reduced medication use and subsequent interventions. This systematic review synthesizes available cost-effectiveness analyses of angle-based MIGS in adults with primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG). Methods: A systematic review of PubMed, Embase, and Scopus databases from inception to 18 March 2026 for full-text, English-language economic evaluations reporting incremental cost-effectiveness/utility ratios (ICER/ICUR) or quality-adjusted life years (QALYs). Methodological quality was assessed using the Drummond checklist and, reporting completeness was assessed using CHEERS 2022. Findings were synthesized descriptively by intervention and comparator group, distinguishing standalone procedures from those combined with cataract surgery. No certainty assessment was undertaken. Results: Sixteen model-based economic evaluations encompassing 39 comparisons were included, with time horizons ranging between 1-year to lifetime. Drummond scores ranged from 8.0-9.5/10. Reported findings generally favored angle-based MIGS compared with medical therapy or cataract surgery (CS) alone, with 61.5% of comparisons categorized as cost-effective and 33.3% as economically dominant. The remaining 5.1% (n = 2) evaluations were not cost-effective at the stated willingness-to-pay thresholds. Of 19 evaluations derived from 10 studies, implant-based procedures combined with CS dominated CS alone across 7 evaluations and was cost-effective across 10 evaluations. Reporting of heterogeneity, distributional effects and stakeholder engagement was inconsistent. Evidence was limited by reliance on modelled outcomes, heterogenous assumptions and limited representation of lower-resource settings. Conclusions: Currently, angle-based MIGS procedures are cost-effective for the management of mild-moderate glaucoma as compared to medical therapy or standalone CS. Future research should prioritize head-to-head comparisons of different MIGS devices and focus on evaluating the cost-effectiveness of real-world implementation particularly in lower-resource settings. Funding: National Medical Research Council support for R.E.K.M. Registration: PROSPERO CRD420261462492.
