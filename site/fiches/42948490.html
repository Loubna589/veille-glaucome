Question étudiée Résumé PubMed (texte source)

PURPOSE To compare the efficacy of trabeculectomy preceded by simultaneous intravitreal and subconjunctival conbercept administration with that of trabeculectomy combined with 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) following intravitreal conbercept injection in patients with neovascular glaucoma (NVG).

METHODS In this comparative study, 120 patients with NVG were enrolled and allocated to two treatment groups. Regression of neovascularization of the iris (NVI) and neovascularization of the angle (NVA) following conbercept administration was assessed. Additional outcome measures included intraocular pressure (IOP), visual acuity, filtering bleb morphology, use of antiglaucoma medications, surgical success rate, and postoperative complications before and after trabeculectomy.

RESULTS On day 1 after conbercept injection, the group receiving simultaneous intravitreal and subconjunctival conbercept demonstrated significantly greater regression of NVI (p = 0.001) and NVA (p = 0.003) than the comparator group. Across follow-up visits, significant reductions in IOP were observed within both groups (time effect: F = 352.42, p = 0.001). Improvement in visual acuity did not differ significantly between groups (p > 0.05). Significant between-group differences in bleb height were observed at 1, 3, and 6 months after trabeculectomy (p = 0.008, p = 0.001, and p = 0.001, respectively). No significant difference in bleb extent was detected at any postoperative timepoint (F = 2.546, p = 0.080). Bleb vascularity differed significantly between groups throughout follow-up (F = 3.297, p = 0.021). Rates of postoperative complications, antiglaucoma medication use before surgery and within 6 months after injection, and overall surgical success did not differ significantly between groups (all p > 0.05).

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

CONCLUSION In patients with NVG undergoing trabeculectomy, simultaneous intravitreal and subconjunctival administration of conbercept was associated with more rapid regression of anterior-segment neovascularization and more favorable postoperative bleb characteristics. However, no significant between-group differences were observed in 6-month IOP control or surgical success. The potential benefits of the combined administration strategy appear to be mainly related to early neovascular regression and bleb morphology, and their significance for long-term surgical outcomes remains to be determined.
