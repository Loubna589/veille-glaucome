Question étudiée Résumé PubMed (texte source)

INTRODUCTION Conventional clinic measurements of intraocular pressure (IOP) rely upon readings taken in the sitting position, which is then established as a treatment target in patients with glaucoma. However, a single office-based measurement may not capture posture-related IOP elevations occurring during daily activities and sleep. Postural changes in IOP, or postural IOP variation, has been shown to correlate with both glaucoma severity and progression. Glaucoma procedures alter aqueous outflow through differing mechanisms and may consequently influence these posture-related IOP responses. This systematic review therefore aims to characterise the available evidence regarding changes in postural IOP variation following glaucoma surgical and laser procedures and to identify areas requiring further investigation.

Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article, Review

METHODS A comprehensive literature search of the PubMed, MEDLINE, Current Contents, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL) databases was performed alongside subsequent hand searches, with a combination of keywords and relevant MeSH terms, from database inception till 3 May 2025. Studies performed in adult patients with open-angle glaucoma undergoing glaucoma procedures, including laser and surgery, were included for consideration. Outcome measures included postural IOP variation for various postures, compared pre and post-procedure. Postures included the sitting, supine, dependent lateral decubitus position (DLDP) and non-DLDP positions.

RESULTS Initial literature review yielded 1200 results. 9 studies were included in the final analysis, of which 4 studies reported outcomes from patients who underwent trabeculectomy surgery, 2 from patients who underwent minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries (MIGS) and 3 from patients who underwent laser trabeculoplasty. All studies had a minimum post-operative follow-up duration of 3 months. Following trabeculectomy surgery, the greatest pre- to post-operative reduction in postural IOP variation (from the sitting to DLDP position) was from 4.69 +/- 3.37 mmHg to 1.39 +/- 1.77 mmHg (p < 0.001), at 3 months. Among studies on MIGS, the greatest pre- to post-operative reduction in postural IOP variation (from the sitting to DLDP position) was observed in patients following XEN45 Gel Stent implantation - from 4.47+/-3.06 mmHg, to 2.53+/-1.70 mmHg, at 1 month post-operatively. Laser trabeculoplasty demonstrated the least consistent outcomes in post-operative reduction in postural IOP variation, across the studies reviewed. Amongst all interventions, trabeculectomy appeared most consistent in reducing postural IOP variation, with all studies demonstrating a decrease by the last follow-up timepoint. Across studies, the greatest change in postural IOP variation appeared to be between the sitting-to-DLDP position, for all procedures and at all timepoints, both pre- and post- intervention. Changes in post-operative postural IOP variation may also be an early indicator of bleb failure following trabeculectomy surgery.

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

CONCLUSION Significant reduction in postural IOP variation is consistently observed following trabeculectomy surgery in eyes with open angle glaucoma and n​ormal tension glaucoma. MIGS procedures, including iStent inject and XEN Gel-stent implantation, demonstrate only a non-statistically significant reduction in postural IOP variation post-operatively. Postural IOP variation may thus be a useful surrogate efficacy outcome measure following trabeculectomy and MIGS procedures in glaucoma.
