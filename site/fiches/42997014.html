Question étudiée Résumé PubMed (texte source)

OBJECTIVE The visual field (VF) test results of many eyes with glaucoma progress despite treatment. This suggests that some eyes are either untreated or that the management of intraocular pressure does not influence the outcome. In this work, we explore whether future VF parameters can be predicted from a baseline OCT-retinal nerve fiber layer (OCT-RNFL) scan using a deep learning model.

Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article

DESIGN Retrospective multicenter cohort study with external validation.
PARTICIPANTS A total of 1792 eyes from 1610 patients were used for model development. External validation was performed on 150 eyes from a second center (Zeiss Cirrus OCT) and 280 eyes from a third center (Heidelberg Spectralis OCT).
METHODS A Vision Transformer (ViT)-based regression model was trained on baseline OCT-RNFL scans to predict 3 key VF indices (follow-up interval: 4.74 ± 2.59 years). Model performance was evaluated using regression metrics with 95% confidence intervals. Saliency maps were generated to identify OCT-RNFL regions for making decisions in predicting functional visual status.
MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES Future VF indices, including mean deviation (MD), pattern standard deviation (PSD), and Visual Field Index (VFI), were predicted and evaluated using mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared error (RMSE).

RESULTS The model achieved MAE of 3.23 (2.91-3.56), 1.76 (1.61-1.93), and 1.14 (1.03-1.27) for future VFI, MD, and PSD in internal validation, with comparable performance in external data sets (Zeiss OCT: 3.19 [2.73-3.66], 1.92 [1.69-2.16], and 1.11 [0.95-1.29]; Heidelberg OCT: 4.14 [3.82-4.46], 2.71 [2.51-2.91], and 1.37 [1.21-1.55]). The corresponding RMSE values reflect similar trends across the internal and external data sets. Saliency maps revealed that the inner and outer RNFL layers were key structures in driving the model's predictions.
