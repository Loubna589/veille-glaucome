Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article

DESIGN Retrospective cohort study.
PARTICIPANTS Children ≤18 years of age seen at a tertiary care children's hospital between 2009 and 2025 who were prescribed any type of steroid and had a documented intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement within 1 to 60 days of steroid initiation. Children with any IOP measurement >21 mmHg recorded prior to or on the day of steroid initiation were excluded.
METHODS Steroid type, route of administration, and patient characteristics, including comorbidities, were extracted from the electronic medical record. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression models were used to identify factors independently associated with ocular hypertension after steroid use.
MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES Ocular hypertension among children who underwent IOP measurement after steroid initiation, defined as IOP >21 mmHg measured within 1 to 60 days of steroid initiation.

Résultats disponibles Résumé PubMed (texte source)

RESULTS Of 5,687 children included (mean age at steroid initiation 8.8 years; 54.4% male), 1,034 (18.2%) developed ocular hypertension. The most frequently prescribed steroid was fluticasone (20.0%), and the most common route of administration was inhaled/intranasal (24.8%). In multivariable analysis, glaucoma conferred the highest odds of ocular hypertension after steroid use (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 6.77; 95% CI, 5.21-8.79), followed by prednisone relative to dexamethasone (aOR, 3.37; 95% CI, 2.30-4.93), juvenile arthritis (aOR, 1.56; 95% CI, 1.09-2.24), and uveitis (aOR, 1.52; 95% CI, 1.16-1.99). Compared with topical administration, ocular (aOR, 3.57; 95% CI, 2.39-5.33) and intravenous (aOR, 2.63; 95% CI, 1.78-3.89) routes each independently conferred higher odds of ocular hypertension. Age, sex, race, ethnicity, diabetes, and asthma were not independently associated with ocular hypertension.

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

CONCLUSIONS Nearly 1 in 5 children with an IOP measurement within 1 to 60 days of steroid initiation developed ocular hypertension. Glaucoma, uveitis, juvenile arthritis, and non-topical routes of administration-particularly ocular and intravenous-were the strongest risk factors, highlighting the importance of ophthalmic monitoring in children receiving glucocorticoid therapy.
