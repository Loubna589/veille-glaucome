Question étudiée Résumé PubMed (texte source)

BACKGROUND Alopecia significantly affects psychological well-being and social functioning. Standard treatments, like minoxidil and finasteride, are limited by delayed efficacy, need for lifelong use, and adverse effects. Prostaglandin analogs (PGAs), originally developed for ocular hypertension, have shown promise in promoting hair growth. This study compares their effects with minoxidil in a murine model.

Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article

MATERIALS AND METHODS After Institutional Animal Ethics Committee approval, 40 male C57BL/6 mice (aged 6-7 weeks, weighing 20-25 g) were randomized into five groups ( n = 8 in each group): (1) vehicle control, (2) minoxidil 5%, (3) latanoprost 0.005%, (4) bimatoprost 0.03%, and (5) travoprost 0.004%. Drugs were applied topically to shaved dorsal skin daily for 28 days. Hair growth was assessed by follicle density, bulb diameter, and area of regrowth using ImageJ from photographs taken on days 1, 7, 15, and 28.

RESULTS All PGAs significantly increased hair density compared to vehicle control (vehicle control: 1.50 ± 0.55 vs. latanoprost: 7.50 ± 1.69, bimatoprost: 7.75 ± 2.87, travoprost: 6.87 ± 3.40; P = 0.001). Latanoprost and bimatoprost showed higher hair density than minoxidil (3.75 ± 2.87; P = 0.038 and 0.010, respectively). Hair follicle diameter was greater with PGAs (latanoprost: 53.12 ± 5.30, bimatoprost: 51.87 ± 7.52, travoprost: 48.12 ± 3.47 µm) versus vehicle control (27.91 ± 5.57 µm, P = 0.001) and minoxidil (38.12 ± 5.46 µm, P = 0.001). Bimatoprost showed the greatest regrowth by day 28, while latanoprost demonstrated earlier onset by day 15. PGAs also caused darker pigmentation and longer hair.
