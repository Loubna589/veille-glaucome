Type d'étude et méthode Résumé PubMed (texte source)PubMed (types de publication)

Type(s) de publication déclaré(s) par PubMed : Journal Article

MATERIALS AND METHODS This retrospective consecutive interventional case series included 15 eyes of 15 pediatric patients who underwent Paul glaucoma implant surgery at a tertiary ophthalmic center in Azerbaijan between January 2023 and December 2025. The primary outcome was change in intraocular pressure from baseline through 6 months. Secondary outcomes included glaucoma medication use, complete success, additional surgical intervention, and postoperative complications. Paired intraocular pressure comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

Population et participants (phrases du résumé) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

This retrospective consecutive interventional case series included 15 eyes of 15 pediatric patients who underwent Paul glaucoma implant surgery at a tertiary ophthalmic center in Azerbaijan between January 2023 and December 2025.
Median age at surgery was 4.8 years (interquartile range [IQR], 2.3 to 10.2 y), and 9 patients (60.0%) were male.
At 6 months, 14 eyes (93.3%) achieved complete success without glaucoma medications.

RESULTS Median age at surgery was 4.8 years (interquartile range [IQR], 2.3 to 10.2 y), and 9 patients (60.0%) were male. Median baseline intraocular pressure was 33.0 mmHg (IQR, 30.0 to 35.0 mmHg), and all eyes were receiving 3 topical glaucoma medications. Median intraocular pressure decreased to 13.0 mmHg at week 1 (P<0.001), 22.0 mmHg at month 1 (P=0.004), 15.0 mmHg at month 3 (P=0.004), and 12.0 mmHg at month 6 (P<0.001), corresponding to an approximate 64% mean reduction from baseline at 6 months. At 6 months, 14 eyes (93.3%) achieved complete success without glaucoma medications. One eye (6.7%) required tube revision. No implant-related sight-threatening complications were documented.

Conclusion des auteurs (citée telle quelle, non évaluée par l'outil) Résumé PubMed (texte source)

CONCLUSIONS In this early real-world pediatric series, the Paul glaucoma implant was associated with substantial intraocular pressure reduction and a high rate of medication-free control at 6 months. Longer prospective comparative studies are required to confirm durability of pressure control and late safety outcomes.
